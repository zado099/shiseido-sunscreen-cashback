## Which Shiseido sunscreen stick are you actually buying?

Two product families get called the same thing by shoppers, and they're genuinely different.

**The US version is now SPF 60+.** Shiseido's current US stick is the **Ultimate Sun Protector Clear Stick SPF 60+**, sold in 0.7 oz / 20 g and a 2.11 oz jumbo. Shiseido describes it as the upgrade to the previous stick, with the SPF raised to 60+ and eight hours of hydration. Active filters in the 60+ version: avobenzone 2.5%, homosalate 11%, octisalate 5%, octocrylene 9%.

**The SPF 50+ stick is still what most of the world gets.** Shiseido sells it as the **Expert Sun Protector Clear Stick SPF 50+** in the UK, as **Suncare Clear Stick SPF50+** in Spain, as **Perfect Sun Protector Clear Stick** in Thailand and Malaysia, and as the **Perfect Sun Protector Stick** in Japan — where it's rated SPF50+ PA++++ and launched in March 2025. The older US SKU, the **Clear Sunscreen Stick SPF 50+**, is still findable at some US retailers, and Shiseido's US site has listed it in a duo set.

Practically: if you're in the US buying new, you're getting the SPF 60+ in a clear stick. If you're buying from an international retailer or picking one up in Tokyo, you're getting SPF 50+ with a graded PA++++ rating on the label instead.

That difference matters less than it looks. Japan caps printed SPF claims at "50+" regardless of the measured value and adds a graded UVA scale, while the US allows higher integers and marks UVA protection only as a broad-spectrum pass or fail. An SPF50+ PA++++ stick is not a weaker product than an SPF 60+ stick. The two labels are answering different regulatory questions.

Both versions share the same core features, though — and both are water-resistant for 80 minutes, dermatologist-tested, ophthalmologist-tested, and non-comedogenic. The SPF 50+ version is explicitly free of oxybenzone and octinoxate; the SPF 60+ version is free of oxybenzone, octinoxate, and parabens.

## Inside the tube: what SynchroShield actually changes

The selling point on both versions is **SynchroShield**, which Shiseido breaks into two mechanisms:

- **WetForce** — negative ions in the formula combine with positive ions in sweat and water to build an additional protective layer. In practice, getting wet or sweaty makes the film more effective rather than stripping it.

- **HeatForce** — a heat-sensing component activates on hot days after application, forming a stronger barrier.

Shiseido's own instructions are specific about activation: HeatForce kicks in on hot days once the product is on your skin, and WetForce needs the applied area exposed to water or sweat for about 30 minutes. So the "gets stronger in water" claim isn't instant, and it isn't a reason to skip reapplying.

The skincare side is 65% of the formula in the SPF 50+ version per Shiseido's UK and Spain pages, built around argan oil for hydration, ProfenseCL (licorice extract plus soy lecithin) for the appearance of uneven tone, and — in the SPF 50+ version — NatureSurgeComplex against pollutant-driven oxidation. The SPF 60+ version carries the same headline ingredients and adds clinical claims: improved plumpness and even tone at two weeks, improved fine lines and smoothness at four, from a clinical test of 30 women.

One thing in the label that's easy to skim past: **"Very water resistant" is not the same as waterproof for a day.** Shiseido's own guidance says reapply at least every two hours, and more often after swimming, sweating, or towel drying, even for a water-resistant formula.

## How to use it without under-applying

Sticks have a specific failure mode, and it's the reason some people finish a stick and still get sunburnt cheeks.

**The swipe feels like coverage and isn't.** SPF is measured in a lab at an application density of 2 mg per square centimetre. A quick zig-zag across your forehead doesn't get close to that. Shiseido's own usage notes say to apply generously, and warn that reducing the quantity significantly lowers the level of protection. If the stick is your only sunscreen, go over each area several times — enough that you can see a visible layer before blending. That's uncomfortable and it's roughly what the tested dose looks like.

**As a top-up, the swipe is fine.** Where the format earns its place is reapplication at 2pm on top of an existing morning layer, over or under makeup, without spreading cream over your foundation. Shiseido's UK consumer test of 109 women found 89% reported an invisible finish, 91% found it convenient and easy to apply to the face, and 92% considered it mess-free on top of makeup.

**Around the eyes and nose, use a finger.** The stick won't fit the contours. Both the US and international instructions say to rub a little onto your fingertips and blend it in for those areas.

**Take the stick out about a centimetre, not more.** Shiseido warns that winding it up too far risks snapping it or jamming the mechanism.

**Wash it off with your normal cleanser.** No oil cleanser required — the brand's own direction is regular cleansing product.
